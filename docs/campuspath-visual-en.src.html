CampusPath V4.1
HKUST × Google Hackathon · Gemini Enterprise for Higher Education

Turn who a student wants to become
into what they can do today.

CampusPath is a long-horizon AI agent system that accompanies a student from enrolment to graduation. It connects the student's authorised growth record, the registry system, the LMS, the course catalogue, their personal calendar and both on- and off-campus opportunities — then organises fragmented resources into a multi-timescale, executable campus growth pathway that recalibrates as things change and never overloads them.

Positioning, in one sentence
Continuously integrate trustworthy on- and off-campus resources with the student's real feedback, and dynamically work backwards from who the student wants to become to a campus growth pathway that is executable now and recalibrated in real time as things change.

01 · Why this project

The real problem is not a lack of resources — it is that resources never become a pathway

Universities are full of courses, competitions, labs, exchanges, talks, clubs, startup support and career opportunities — but they are highly fragmented. On top of a heavy academic load, students have to search, verify, judge, plan and track all of it themselves, at a very high cognitive cost. Many opportunities are missed not because the student was not capable, but because they found out too late, could not tell whether they were eligible, did not know how to combine things, or made a plan that did not match their real energy.

The six long-term costs this project reduces

COST 1
Discovery cost
Not knowing which trustworthy opportunities exist
COST 2
Comprehension cost
Seeing an opportunity but not knowing its actual eligibility rules or value
COST 3
Planning cost
Not knowing how courses, experiences and opportunities form a pathway with real dependencies
COST 4
Execution cost
Bookmarking a lot, but never converting it into actions, materials and calendar entries
COST 5
Correction cost
Nothing accumulates afterwards, so the system keeps scheduling things that did not work or did not fit
COST 6
Capacity cost
Not knowing how coursework, life and growth tasks fit into a real calendar — leading to clashes, overload, or squeezed rest
Why a one-off career plan is not enough: A student's abilities, goals, energy and external opportunities all change. An internship you do not qualify for in year one may fit perfectly in year three; a direction that seemed appealing may turn out not to suit you after one hands-on experience. So the genuinely useful product is not a static roadmap — it is a growth system that can keep sensing, remembering, verifying and replanning.
02 · Purpose and final product positioning

A campus growth operating system — not another job aggregator

Final product positioning
A dynamic campus growth operating system, powered by Gemini Enterprise and centred on the student's own choices: it turns long-term direction into near-term action, turns real action into evidence and memory, and then uses that new evidence to continuously recalibrate the next stage of the pathway.
Spec V4.1 · §2.1 (translated from the Chinese source)

The core loop

Profile + Education + Calendar/Capacity State→ Goal→Requirement→Gap→ Course & Opportunity→Constraint-safe Plan→ Consent-based Action→Reflection & Evidence→ Confirmed Profile/Memory Update→Replan

Two-sided value: what the student gets, what the university gets

The brief is Gemini Enterprise for Higher Education. The party that actually deploys and pays is the university, so the product must make both value propositions true at once.

FOR STUDENTS
Long-term direction, worked backwards into something executable now that does not squeeze out life
Every recommendation states which gap it closes, what evidence it produces, and why now.
FOR THE UNIVERSITY
Turn resources the university already funds heavily into measurable, improvable, attributable growth assets
And, for the first time, show the university which real student needs its resource pool does not cover.
How "university resources" are defined here: Any opportunity a student can access because of their enrolled status counts as a university resource. Alumni referrals, employers recruiting on campus, partner-organisation places, inter-university consortium events — all of these exist because of the university's brand and network, and do not stop counting just because the organiser sits outside the campus. So this product does not split resources into on-campus versus off-campus. It only distinguishes whether the student found it, qualified for it, and used it.
03 · Who this is for

Five goal-oriented groups — not five identity labels

These five are grouped by where a student wants to go, not by undergraduate / master's / doctoral status or nationality, and they are not permanent labels. A student can hold two candidate directions at once and shift the weighting as they gain experience.

MODE 1
Employment & career development
Entering a target company, industry, role or public-service field
System focus: role requirements, skill evidence, projects, internships, portfolio, network, recruiting rhythm
MODE 2
Academia & postgraduate study
Master's, PhD, entering a research field, finding a supervisor
System focus: course foundations, research methods, labs, paper reading, research experience, references, application timeline
MODE 3
Entrepreneurship & innovation
Starting a project, validating a need, joining an incubator, forming a team
System focus: problem discovery, technical/business capability, competitions, prototypes, user validation, mentors, funding, team
MODE 4
Interests & personal growth
Art, sport, language, public service, creative work or other long-term interests
System focus: capability growth, community, work produced, experience quality, sustainable commitment — without forcing professionalisation
MODE 5
Exploration & undecided
Currently unsure, hoping to understand themselves through practice
System focus: low-cost, varied exploratory experiments; updating interests and goal confidence from real experience
A CROSS-CUTTING CONSTRAINT — NOT A SIXTH GROUP
Context Pack
International status, financial situation, caring responsibilities, health and energy, commuting, language, accessibility needs
Injected as a personal constraint that every development mode must respect, rather than duplicating a whole new set of agents

Career Path Pack — the goal model is pluggable, not a duplicated agent: What differs between development paths is only "what this path requires, what evidence counts, and on what timeline" — not the agent structure. So it is abstracted into a versioned rule pack, structurally identical to the Context Pack.
The MVP ships exactly one pack: undergrad-direct-employment. A second pack, phd-to-industry (doctoral graduates moving into industry), is reserved at the interface level — if that pack is not delivered, the MVP is unaffected, the front end does not show it, and no material claims support for that path.
04 · Boundaries

What this product is not

Explicit exclusions matter as much as the function list — they rule out whole implementation routes from the start.

  • ×Not a "success predictor" that guarantees entry to a particular company or university
  • ×Not an automated decision system that makes irreversible life decisions on a student's behalf
  • ×Not a system that measures growth by activity count, busyness or CV length
  • ×Not a surveillance tool that scrapes personal or platform data without authorisation
  • ×Not a tool for the university to inspect students' private thoughts or control their choices
  • ×Not a medical device, a psychological diagnostic tool, or a suicide-risk prediction system
  • ×Not a monitoring system that reports on a student to the university based on calendar gaps, sleep patterns or exercise minutes

06 · The agent system

6 semantic agents, 2 runtimes, 9 deterministic services

Design principle: do not wrap every function in an agent. Use an agent only where there is an independent objective, genuine semantic judgement, distinct tooling or distinct state. Repeatable logic — date comparison, eligibility rules, de-duplication, capacity checks, scoring — should be deterministic code.

The core architectural principle in V4.1: A5 is the only agent in the system that makes trade-offs. A1 / A2 / A3 / A4 only assemble facts and candidates cleanly; they do not rank. This gives "why was this recommended" exactly one source of explanation, and lets every agent's output contract be evaluated independently.

Three uses of ADK workflow agents

These are workflow constructs inside an agent, not new deployment units: the agent count stays at 6, the runtime count stays at 2, and the number of governed objects in the Registry and Gateway is unchanged.

S1 · ParallelAgent
Inside A5: parallel Plan A/B/C
Balanced / Ambitious / Low-load are generated in parallel under three different intensity constraints, so each plan gets independent, focused reasoning without the latency adding up.
Implements F12
S2 · LoopAgent
Inside A5: constraint-repair loop
Generate plan → Rules validation → if capacity, protected blocks or prerequisites are violated, regenerate carrying the violation reason (max 3 rounds).
Makes Capacity Violation = 0 and Protected Block Violation = 0 loop invariants rather than aspirations
S3 · ParallelAgent
Inside A4: isolated multi-source extraction
Sources are extracted in parallel, with each source's untrusted content isolated in its own sub-context — an injection in one source cannot contaminate another.
Implements F09, and is simultaneously a security measure
08 · Agent security contracts

Moving privacy from "we worded the prompt carefully" to "the architecture cannot do it"

These four are deployment contracts, not design suggestions: each has a corresponding automated test, and a failing test fails the build.

§8.9.1
A4 untrusted-content isolation
① External text is always passed as a user-role data block with boundary markers, and never concatenated into the system prompt;
② the tool allow-list contains only read_source and emit_opportunity_draft — no student-data reads, no publishing rights, no outbound writes;
③ output must pass a deterministic schema gate before entering the review queue; failures are discarded and alerted, never fed back to the LLM for a retry.
Threat-model conclusion: even if A4 is fully hijacked, the maximum impact is "a draft that a human reviewer rejects"
§8.9.2
A1 output type boundary
A1 touches both the student's most private content (reflection text) and the quality signal that eventually reaches the university. Its output to the Aggregation Service can only be a structured EventQualityFeedback — free text is not accepted at the interface type level — and A1 holds no tool permission to write to the aggregation domain.
Private Reflection Exposure = 0 is guaranteed jointly by a data-type boundary and a tool-permission boundary
§8.9.3
Mandatory validation_id binding on A5
Every Rules validation returns a validation_id. Every PlanItem and every eligibility conclusion A5 emits must carry the matching ID, and the API layer rejects anything missing or forged.
Guarantees that eligibility, prerequisites, deadlines, capacity and protected blocks can never be overridden by model output
§8.9.4
Zero LLM on the wellbeing path
The five signal determinations, the reminder copy and the outreach email content are all produced by deterministic code and fixed templates. The reasoning: these five determinations are pure threshold comparisons with no semantic judgement, so an LLM adds no capability and only adds hallucination risk — on the highest-risk data class in the entire product.
The only LLM permitted on this path is A5 generating a low-load alternative plan, still bound by validation_id

16 · Quantified metrics

The north-star metric and the resource-utilisation scale

North-star metric
Verified Growth Actions (VGA) / Active Student / Month

Actions the student genuinely completed, where evidence or reflection demonstrates value created in skills, knowledge, artefacts, research, network, opportunity progression or goal clarification. VGA does not reward clicks, bookmarks, sign-ups or busyness itself.

Resource-utilisation metrics

VGA measures "how many effective actions the student completed", but the product positioning also claims "university resources are fully used". That layer needs its own scale — otherwise the claim cannot be checked during judging.

MetricDefinitionWhat it proves
Eligible Opportunity
Discovery Rate		 Of the resources for which a student was Eligible now during a term, the proportion they actually saw Quantifies discovery cost: how many opportunities were missed not because the student did not qualify, but because they were never seen
Discovered-to-
Action Rate		 Of the resources seen while eligible, the proportion converted into a bookmark, sign-up, pathway addition or completed action Quantifies execution cost: whether the system turns discovery into action rather than just a bigger bookmark list
Gap Coverage by
Available Resources		 Of the gaps in a student's Gap Map, the proportion coverable by at least one resource in the pool A resource-supply insight fed back to the university — a management input no university can obtain today

How these three metrics reach the university

These metrics must reach the Career Center, or they are just numbers the team scores itself with and produce no management value for the university. But the delivery path is bound by exactly the same privacy discipline as activity quality feedback: what travels is an aggregate conclusion, never any individual student's data. These are flows E32 / E33 on the main architecture diagram.

STAGE 1 · PER-STUDENT COMPUTATION
Happens inside the student's private data domain
Uses that student's four-state eligibility results, exposure records, ActionEvents and Gap Map to compute a single de-identified MetricTuple.
Raw exposure and action records never leave the student's data domain; the tuple carries no student_id on exit
STAGE 2 · ANONYMOUS AGGREGATION
Aggregation Service (deterministic, zero LLM)
Applies a minimum sample threshold and small-cell suppression to MetricTuple[], producing a ResourceCoverageAggregate.
Cross-tabulation below the threshold is forbidden, so repeated filtering cannot shrink a cell to an identifiable size
STAGE 3 · UNIVERSITY VIEW
Career Center Insights
Global and cohort trend views. The university sees aggregate conclusions only; neither the interface nor the backend offers any way to look up an individual.
Converges with the Equity & Reach Insights module

What the MetricTuple carries when it leaves the student data domain

Carries
  • ✓period, coarse cohort_dims (school / year / development mode)
  • ✓eligible_count, seen_count, acted_count
  • ✓gap_total, gap_covered
  • ✓The requirement category of uncovered gaps (e.g. "user research practice") — not the gap text itself
Explicitly does not carry
  • ×student_id, name, student number
  • ×Profile fields, goal text, specific gap descriptions
  • ×Reflection text, private notes
  • ×Calendar data, or any wellbeing field

17 · Latency budget

Latency is a design constraint, not an afterthought

If the multi-agent chain ran entirely serially, a single interaction could take 20–45 seconds — unacceptable for an interactive web app and for a timed demo.

MeasureHowEffect
A0 deterministic routingKnown intents dispatch directly without LLM orchestrationCovers ~90% of requests, saves one LLM round trip
A1 / A2 / A3 in parallelThey have no interdependency, so they run under an ADK ParallelAgentThree serial calls become one parallel call
RequirementGraph cachingNot recomputed while the goal is unchangedA3 skipped in the normal case
Zero-LLM deterministic servicesCapacity / Rules / ComposerThese stages run in milliseconds

18 · Mapping to the judging criteria

Gemini Enterprise for Higher Education

APPLICATION NOVELTY
One continuous loop
Long-term goals, prerequisite dependencies, dynamic eligibility, personal energy, opportunity quality, real actions and long-term memory all live in a single continuous loop — not a one-off assessment or a job aggregator.
REAL-WORLD VIABILITY
Hybrid architecture plus two-sided value
A standalone student app plus Gemini Enterprise agents plus Moodle / Workspace / Career Center / BYO-MCP; the university replaces none of its existing systems. The gap-coverage metric gives the university a resource-planning insight.
QUANTIFIABLE IMPACT
A reproducible gold-set evaluation
Measures time to discovery, four-state eligibility accuracy, course constraint satisfaction, plan executability, action conversion, repeated low-value recommendations, replanning quality, and the three resource-utilisation metrics.
ECOSYSTEM EXECUTION
The full ADK workflow-agent set
ADK multi-agent, all three workflow agent types (sequential / parallel / loop), Agent Runtime, Memory Bank, Registry, Gateway, Workspace APIs, Cloud Run and a self-built MCP.
The strongest security narrative: we deliberately do not use AI where it matters most. Zero LLM across the entire wellbeing path, three untrusted-content isolation contracts on A4, and a type-level boundary between private reflection text and university-facing data — this is a threat model that can be explained directly to judges, rather than a line saying "we care about privacy".
